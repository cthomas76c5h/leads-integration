# Lead delivery: the one-page summary

## The problem

Law firms pay us for one thing: **every new client who calls, reaching them in
time.** Today we cannot show that we do it.

- **Leads go missing without anyone noticing.** When a firm's CRM connection stops, nothing breaks; the leads simply stop arriving, and we usually hear from the firm, days later.
- **Speed is not measured.** A caller who is not called back within the hour often calls the next firm. A lead that reaches the CRM a day late has kept its paperwork and lost its client.
- **Every firm is set up differently.** Firms use 4,390 different names for kinds of call, which boil down to about a dozen. Most of our support work is the same few needs expressed thousands of ways.
- **Our billing cannot explain itself.** Minutes with a hidden multiplier, rates that differ account by account, and free calls decided by a typed label. Nothing on the invoice shows what the firm got.
- **Terms of service locked people out.** Every edit, even a grammar fix, forced everyone to accept again before signing in.
- **Nobody owns a lead from start to finish.** Five systems grew one at a time; problems are fixed where they appear and come back elsewhere.

## What we will do

1. **Name one owner** for every lead, start to finish, judged by one number: the share of leads that reached a person at the firm in time.
2. **Measure first.** Within two weeks, record what happens to every lead and why, and alert someone the moment a firm's connection stops.
3. **Never lose a lead silently.** Automatic retries, a backup route each firm agrees in advance (usually a text to its intake team), and leads that missed a stopped connection sent on once it is back.
4. **Standardise.** A dozen standard kinds of call for every firm (firms keep their own wording), one way to describe a lead from phone and chat, and six supported CRMs with everything else through Zapier.
5. **Bill on value.** Three packages (Answer, Deliver, Insight), priced per real conversation; sales calls, wrong numbers and instant hang-ups are free; one record behind every invoice; each firm's new bill checked against its old one for two months before anyone pays differently.
6. **Fix the terms of service.** One version for everyone; people are asked to accept again only when a change concerns them, with notice first; every acceptance recorded.

## When

| Time | What changes |
|---|---|
| Weeks 1–2 | Real numbers on lost and late leads; alerts on stopped connections; terms changes ship without locking anyone out |
| Weeks 3–8 | No lead lost without a trace; late leads counted; every firm receives a weekly report of its calls |
| Months 3–5 | One standard lead everywhere; firms choose which calls go where; the new bill runs beside the old |
| After | Packages launched; firms set themselves up; other software can receive our leads; a package for trades businesses |

## Why it matters for the business

- **Retention:** firms can see that every lead reached them, in time.
- **Margin:** support work stops growing with every new firm.
- **Predictable revenue:** standard packages, invoices anyone can check.
- **A unique asset:** a record of what callers need, across thousands of firms.
- **Growth:** almost everything already works for trades businesses, not only law firms.

## What we need from leadership

1. Name the owner of lead delivery.
2. Agree the time limits per kind of call (our proposal: 15 minutes for emergencies, 1 hour for new clients).
3. Approve the billing direction, starting with two months of side-by-side bills.
4. Approve counsel's review of the terms-of-service wording for firm admins accepting on the firm's behalf.
