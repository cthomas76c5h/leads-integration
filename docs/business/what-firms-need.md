# What firms need

What law firms need from us, as their use of the service shows it. The figures are
rough and rounded: they describe the shape of the business, not exact measurements.
It informs the [commercial case](commercial-case.md).

## Someone to answer when they cannot

About 80% of calls come during regular business hours, while attorneys are in court,
with clients, or simply busy. Firms rely on us as the receptionist for the calls they
cannot take. After-hours cover matters, but it is the smaller part.

## A message they can act on

Firms judge us by the message we take. Most of what they raise with us, well over
half, is about a message: a detail wrong or missing, or a call handled differently
than they wanted. Getting the details right matters more to them than anything else we
do.

## The right person, reached in time

When we try to put a caller straight through, roughly two times in three nobody at the
firm picks up. Firms need a dependable way for the right person to hear about the call
when they cannot answer, and someone to follow up until they do.

## Information where they already look

Firms read their calls in the email or text we send. Fewer than one in ten leads is
ever opened in the portal, and most portal accounts are never activated. Anything we
want them to see (a message, an urgent call, proof that a lead arrived, a weekly
picture) has to reach them by email or text.

## To pay for calls that matter

Around 40% of the calls we answer are worth nothing to the firm: hang-ups, wrong
numbers, sales calls. Firms expect not to pay for those, and to understand what they
are paying for.

## Calls sorted the way they think about them

Only about one call in five is a new client. The rest are messages for the firm,
calls for a named person, calls from courts, insurers and existing clients. Firms
think in these terms, not in the free-text labels each set up over the years.

## Confidence that nothing slips through

Firms rarely ask for this, because they assume it: hardly any complaints are about a
lead that never arrived. They only notice when a caller says nobody got back to them.
Dependable delivery is expected, not bought on its own.

## Who they are

Most firms are small, taking a few dozen calls a month. A fifth of firms account for
about two-thirds of all calls, and that is where most of the value sits. About nine in
ten are law firms; the rest are other businesses using the same service.

## What this means for us

- **Message quality comes first.** It is what firms notice and judge.
- **Reaching someone when the firm cannot answer is the hand-off that matters most.**
- **Proof and dependability belong inside the service,** shown in the email and text firms already read, not sold as a separate product.
- **Billing should follow the calls that matter,** decided by clear rules, not a label chosen during the call.
- **The busiest fifth of firms deserve the closest attention:** they carry most of the volume, and most of the value.
