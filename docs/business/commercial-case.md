# The commercial case

Who buys, what problem they pay to solve, and why they would choose us, answered the
way a sales or product engineer would. The figures are rough and rounded: they
describe the shape of the business, not exact measurements. What firms need is in
[what-firms-need.md](what-firms-need.md); decisions stay in
[answers.md](../lead-delivery/answers.md), and the product and engineering direction
in [strategy.md](../lead-delivery/strategy.md).

## The proposition

> **A dependable receptionist that follows your firm's instructions, knows when to
> involve a person, and makes sure every call reaches its next action.**

Three promises to prove: **consistency** (instructions followed), **accuracy** (the
caller's details right) and **continuity** (someone owns every unfinished call). How we
measure them, and the plan for the year, are in [the next 12 months](next-12-months.md).

- **Customer:** small and growing law firms that cannot take every call while they work.
- **Problem:** calls come in during the working day while attorneys are in court or with clients; when we try to put a caller through, usually nobody picks up; and the firm judges us by the message we leave.
- **Solution:** a managed intake service that answers, follows each firm's own way of handling calls, reaches whoever can act, follows up until someone does, and shows what happened in the email or text the firm already reads.
- **Value:** fewer interruptions, every caller handled the firm's way, and fewer callers lost when nobody could pick up.
- **What we have to prove:** that we handle calls exactly as each firm wants, and that the hand-off still works when the firm cannot answer. Cheaper AI calls are not the point.

Lead delivery is not the product. It is an invisible part of a service firms already
understand, built on the call center that answers
([where this sits](../../README.md#where-this-sits)).

## What we changed our minds about

| We assumed | We now think |
|---|---|
| Lead delivery is the product | Dependable, accurate call handling is the core service |
| Lost leads are the main pain | The message we leave is the pain firms feel |
| After-hours cover is the main use | Daytime overflow matters more |
| CRM integrations are central | Most firms work from email and text |
| Firms want a portal or dashboard | Information has to reach them where they already look |
| Reliability is a premium tier | Reliability belongs in the core service |

## The questions

### Who buys?

- **Small firms (most of our customers):** the owner attorney signs, and is also the person juggling the calls.
- **Growing firms:** the managing partner signs, on the word of the office or intake manager, who is blamed when a call goes wrong.
- **Larger firms:** an operations or intake director, interested in response times and consistency. Few today.
- **Other businesses:** a small share of customers are not law firms, served by the same service.

A fifth of firms take about two-thirds of the calls. They are where most of the value,
and most of the risk, sits.

### What is their expensive problem?

Calls they cannot take while they work, handled badly enough that something is lost or
has to be redone. The costliest version is a new client who never hears back and calls
the next firm. But new clients are only about one call in five, so the everyday problem
is broader: every call handled the way the firm wants.

### What do they buy today?

- **From us:** answering, billed by the minute in a way firms find hard to check.
- **From a CRM vendor:** practice management, and increasingly the vendor's own intake tools.
- **From their own staff:** time spent calling back, sorting messages and fixing mistakes.
- **From marketing:** the inquiries themselves. That spend already puts a price on a lead, which anchors our value.

Our first market is the customers we already have: better service and upgrades, not
new logos.

### What makes them buy or upgrade?

- A caller who says nobody got back to them.
- Growth in calls, or a busier season.
- Losing or hiring intake staff.
- Adopting or switching a CRM.
- A bill they disagree with, which can as easily end in a cancellation.

### Why would they choose us?

- **Against another answering service:** we follow your way of handling calls every time, and reach someone when you cannot pick up.
- **Against their CRM's own intake:** we work with whatever system you use, or none, and cover the phone all day.
- **Against another AI receptionist:** we know legal calls, and the hand-off does not stop at "message sent".

Much of this is proven in our lab, not yet in production. Until it ships, we compete as
an answering service on service and price.

### What makes them stay?

- Calls handled their way, week after week.
- Never discovering a caller who slipped through.
- A bill they understand, without paying for calls that are worth nothing to them.
- A picture of their calls that shows the value.

### What will they pay?

About what they pay today for answering, with premiums for what they see directly:
following their intake rules, scheduling, urgent escalation, other languages and
reporting. Dependable delivery is probably part of the core price, not a separate
tier. Test it with two offers at real prices:

- **Offer A:** premium answering with a dependable hand-off and proof.
- **Offer B:** intake coordination sold separately: escalation, ownership of each call, CRM.

Whether firms accept the price tells us more than whether they like the idea. Our
billing plan has a separate delivery tier; if offer A wins, that changes.

### Why do they complain, cancel or upgrade?

**Complaints are mostly about the message we took, not about delivery.** Hardly any
are about a lead that never arrived. Message complaints mostly come down to:

- **A call handled differently from how the firm asked:** booking a consultation, what to tell callers about fees, when to put a call through. The largest group, more than a third.
- **Calls the firm did not want,** or wants credited. About a quarter.
- **A wrong or missing detail.** Only about one in ten.

So the next advantage is **following each firm's rules exactly, every time**, not
hearing callers better.

**Why firms cancel or upgrade is still unknown.** Each answer points somewhere
different:

| If firms leave because of | Then invest in |
|---|---|
| Price | Simpler pricing and cheaper service |
| Calls handled badly | Firm-specific intake rules, review and escalation |
| Missed callers | Reaching someone when the firm cannot answer |
| Too much left for their staff | Workflow automation |
| Their CRM vendor's own intake | Working across systems, and the human side of the service |

## How we would sell it

### Discovery

Ask "walk me through what happens when someone calls your office", then about:

- **Capture:** how many calls go unanswered when they are busy.
- **Handling:** the rules they want followed, and where they get broken.
- **Hand-off:** who needs to know, how fast, and what happens when nobody picks up.
- **Follow-up:** who calls back, and who notices when it slips.
- **Results:** whether they know how many calls became clients.

### The demonstration

Show a busy day, not a dashboard: a call comes in while the attorney is in court; it
is handled the firm's way; the transfer finds nobody; the right person gets a clear
message and a callback is arranged; the firm sees the whole story in its email.

### By segment

| Segment | What we sell | What we show |
|---|---|---|
| Small firm | A receptionist when you cannot pick up, and a bill you can read | A call taken while the attorney is in court |
| Growing firm | Calls handled your way, every time, and nothing slipping through | A missed transfer that still reaches the right person |
| Busiest firms | Intake run like a process: rules, routing, ownership, reporting | Their week of calls, and where time goes |

## What we would deliberately not do

- Compete as a CRM.
- Compete on the cheapest call.
- Build many CRM integrations ahead of demand.
- Sell infrastructure to other providers before anyone asks.
- Market beyond legal yet.

## Experiments

- **Firm rules, applied every time:** do complaints about messages fall?
- **Unanswered transfers:** does a structured fallback and callback take work off the firm?
- **The busiest firms:** will they pay for workflow automation?

## What we know, think, and need to learn

**Fairly sure:**

- Firms pay us to answer calls they cannot take, mostly during the working day.
- Live transfers usually find nobody at the firm.
- Firms complain about messages, mostly about how calls were handled.
- Firms read their calls by email or text, rarely in the portal.
- Many calls are worth nothing to the firm.

**We think, but have not shown:**

- Firm-specific rules matter more than better speech recognition.
- Most firms take their leads by email rather than a CRM.
- Dependable delivery sells better inside the core service than as its own tier.

**Still to learn:**

- Why firms cancel and upgrade.
- How often a caller is actually lost.
- What firms will pay, and for what.
- Whether today's needs match what we have seen, since our view of firms' use is not recent.

## The question this page must answer

> **Which firms will pay materially more for something only we can do, and keep paying
> once the novelty wears off?**

Until that has an answer backed by evidence, this is a hypothesis, not the business
strategy.
